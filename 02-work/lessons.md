# Lessons — mistakes made once, never again

Add to this the day you learn something. Every correction you make to a draft is a rule
your future agent should already know.

## Seeded with universal ones. Replace and extend with your own.

1. **Never let an inflated metric reach a draft.** If you can't defend it under two
   follow-up questions, it isn't yours. `metrics.md` is the firewall.
2. **Under-claiming is also an accuracy failure.** Present the most impressive defensible
   version, not the most modest one.
3. **Thin "why this company" answers lose mission-driven applications.** The fix is
   specificity about the actual day job, not more enthusiasm.
4. **One idea per bullet, metric at the end.** Bullets over two lines get skimmed past.
5. **A domain stated as a hard minimum is a near-hard gate.** Apply anyway only with a
   referral or a genuine bridging artifact.
6. **Free-text application questions are read by humans.** They're an effort and judgment
   signal. Generic answers read as low effort.
7. **De-duplicate story climaxes across an essay set.** Two answers ending on the same win
   reads as one story stretched thin.
8. **Never disclose a current employer's confidential information.** Not in an essay, not
   in an artifact, not "just as context."
9. **Warmth does not predict outcome.** A hiring manager who reaches out personally, a
   "the whole team loved you," ten hours of process — none of it is a decision. Stay warm,
   stay in process, don't let it soften your comp stance.
10. **Summaries should name where the company is headed and how you close that gap**, not
    list your attributes.
11. **Check every proper noun before sending.** Company names, product names, people's
    names. One typo in the name undoes a great artifact.
12. **Test anything you build before you send it.** The live run is usually better than
    the version you imagined.
